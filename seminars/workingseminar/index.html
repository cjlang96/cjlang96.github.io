Memorial Mathematics Working Seminar

Fall Schedule: HH 3017 12 – 1 pm

October 15thTom Baird - The Cohomology of Sheaves
Sheaves and their cohomology play a fundamental role in algebra, geometry, and topology. In this series of lectures, I will introduce these concepts, develop their theory, and explore applications.

In this first lecture, I will explain what a sheaf is and why is interesting, and then define the cohomology of a sheaf.
October 8thDaniel Offin (Queen’s University) - Dynamics and symmetry: minimizing brake orbits
After Newton proposed his universal laws of motion, there was a lively debate in the 18th c concerning the principle of least action championed by Maupertuis and later Euler and Lagrange. This approach was based on Maupertuis’s principle of minimum action: \(\mathrm{inf}_\gamma\int_\gamma pdq\).

Maupertuis’s principle of least action states that in all natural phenomena a quantity called ‘action’ tends to be minimised. We shall give a short history of this principle and its modern formulations culminating in the study of the spectrum of minimizers, bringing together symplectic geometry, dynamical systems and symmetry groups.
